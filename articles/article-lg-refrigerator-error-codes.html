How LG Refrigerator Codes Work

LG uses a fairly consistent naming pattern across its refrigerator lineup, and once you see the logic it stops feeling random. Most codes start with Er for “error” and then a two-letter suffix that names the part: FF is the freezer fan, rF is the fresh-food (refrigerator side) fan, IF is the ice maker fan, and dH is the defrost heater. A few codes break the pattern entirely; OF F isn't an error at all, and CH E or CL E point straight at the sealed refrigerant system. The same codes show up on side-by-side, French door, and top-freezer LG models, so this reference applies no matter which style is sitting in your kitchen.

A couple of things are worth knowing before you reach for a screwdriver:

  • Not every code means the unit is broken. OF F is the single most common false alarm we see: a refrigerator that looks completely dead is often just stuck in showroom mode from the factory, a delivery, or a recent power outage.
  • Fan codes are usually an ice problem before they're a motor problem. Er FF, Er rF, and Er IF almost always start as ice locking a fan blade in place, not a dead motor. A manual defrost clears most of them without a single replacement part.

LG Refrigerator Error Codes: Code by Code

OF F

Demo Mode, Not a Fault (Also Shown as OF OF)

What it means: The refrigerator is in Demo, or Showroom, mode, which shuts off cooling on purpose so the unit can sit on a sales floor without running. It isn't a malfunction, but it looks exactly like one: no cold air, unresponsive-feeling controls, and a fridge that seems to have died overnight.

Check first: Press and hold the Refrigerator and Ice Plus buttons together for about 5 seconds until the display resets to normal operating mode. This setting is sometimes switched on by accident after delivery, a filter change, or a power outage that reset the control board.

If it persists: If OF F won't clear with that button combination, or the fridge still isn't cooling once it's out of demo mode, the problem has moved from a settings issue to a mechanical one and is worth a diagnostic visit.

Er FF / Er rF

Freezer or Fresh-Food Fan Motor Abnormality

What it means: Er FF is the freezer evaporator fan and Er rF is the fresh-food side fan. Both mean the control board sent power to the fan and never got confirmation that it actually spun, typically after waiting roughly 65 seconds for a response.

Check first: Unplug the refrigerator, prop both doors open, and let any built-up ice around the fan blade melt fully; this can take several hours. Ice is by far the most common cause, and a blade that's frozen in place reads to the control board exactly like a dead motor.

If it recurs: If the code comes back once the compartment is back to normal temperature and free of ice, the fan motor itself has failed and needs replacing.

Er IF

Ice Maker Fan Blocked or Failing

What it means: Er IF points at the small fan inside the ice maker compartment that circulates cold air to the ice maker itself. When it can't turn freely, ice production slows down or stops.

Check first: Open the freezer and check the ice maker compartment for a buildup of frost or ice around the fan opening; this is common after a door was left ajar or during humid Orange County summers. Clear any visible ice by hand once the unit is unplugged.

If it recurs: If the fan is clear of ice and the code still appears, the fan motor has likely failed and needs replacement.

Repair cost: $250–$450. Estimates vary by brand, part availability, and diagnosis. Final quote is provided before repair.

Er dH

Failed Defrost Cycle

What it means: Er dH means the automatic defrost cycle ran, but the defrost sensor never reached its target temperature within the allowed time. Either the defrost heater isn't producing heat or the sensor reading it has failed.

Check first: There isn't a reliable DIY fix for this one. Opening the freezer to check for a thick sheet of ice on the evaporator coil behind the back panel can confirm the defrost system isn't doing its job, but testing the heater and sensor takes a multimeter and some disassembly.

Technician needed: Replacing the defrost heater or the defrost sensor typically resolves it.

Repair cost: $150–$300. Estimates vary by brand, part availability, and diagnosis. Final quote is provided before repair.

CH E / CL E

Refrigerant Leak (Serious, EPA-Certified Technician Required)

What it means: CH E and CL E point at the sealed refrigerant system, a leak on the high side or low side of the circuit. Refrigerant loss shows up as a fridge that runs constantly but never gets cold, or a compressor that short-cycles.

What to do: Don't attempt to open the sealed system yourself. Handling refrigerant requires EPA Section 608 certification, and a botched attempt can turn a slow leak into a total loss of the compressor.

Technician needed: A certified technician will pressure-test the system, locate the leak, repair or replace the affected line or component, then evacuate and recharge with the correct refrigerant.

Repair cost: $400–$1,200 depending on where the leak is and how much of the system needs to be rebuilt. Estimates vary by brand, part availability, and diagnosis. Final quote is provided before repair.

Quick Reference: DIY vs Repair

The five codes above cover most of the calls we get, but LG refrigerators use a longer list of two- and three-character codes. Here's the fuller set and what each one is telling you.

Code What it points at DIY step worth trying
OF F / OF OF Demo / Showroom mode (not a fault) Hold Refrigerator + Ice Plus 5 seconds
Er FF Freezer fan motor Manual defrost (unplug, doors open)
Er rF Fresh-food fan motor Manual defrost (unplug, doors open)
Er IF Ice maker compartment fan Clear ice from the compartment
Er dH Failed defrost cycle None reliable; needs a technician
Er CO Main board to display communication Power-cycle once
Er CF Condenser / compressor compartment fan Clear dust and ice from the vents
Er IT Ice maker kit (motor or gears) Clear jams; often needs a new kit
Er dS Defrost sensor None; inspect wiring
Er FS / Er rS Temperature sensor (freezer / fresh food) None; inspect wiring
Er gF Water / ice flow sensor Check water supply and pressure
CH E / CL E Refrigerant leak None; call a certified technician
The reset rule: If a code returns right after a power cycle, or right after you've cleared visible ice, the underlying part has failed rather than glitched. Running the compressor for days against a code like Er CF or CH E can turn a modest repair into a compressor replacement, so don't wait it out.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I reset my LG refrigerator?

Unplug the refrigerator for 30 seconds to a minute, or switch off its breaker for about 5 minutes, then restore power. This clears a code caused by a one-time glitch and lets the control board re-check its sensors on startup. If the same code reappears the moment power is restored, the underlying fault is still there; a reset alone will not fix a locked fan blade, a failed sensor, or a refrigerant leak.

What does OF F mean on my LG refrigerator?

OF F (sometimes shown as OF OF) is Demo or Showroom mode, not a fault. It disables cooling so the unit can sit on a showroom floor without running, and it is often left on by mistake after delivery or a power outage. To exit it, press and hold the Refrigerator and Ice Plus buttons together for about 5 seconds until the display returns to normal. If your fridge is not cooling and shows OF F, this is almost always the fix, no technician needed.

What does Er FF or Er rF mean on an LG refrigerator?

Er FF is the freezer fan motor and Er rF is the fresh-food (refrigerator side) fan motor. Both mean the control board is not getting feedback from that fan after about 65 seconds, almost always because ice has built up around the blade and locked it in place. Unplug the refrigerator, leave both doors open, and let the ice melt for several hours. If the code returns once the unit is back to normal operating temperature and free of ice, the fan motor itself has failed and needs to be replaced.
