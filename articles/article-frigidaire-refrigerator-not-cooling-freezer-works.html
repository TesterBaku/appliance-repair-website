Frigidaire Fridge Not Cooling, Freezer Works? Check the Damper

Home › Blog › Frigidaire Fridge Not Cooling, Freezer Works

The freezer is solid and the ice cream is fine, but the milk on the refrigerator side is lukewarm. On a side-by-side that split usually means the cold is being made but not delivered, and one small part that controls the delivery, the air damper, is a known suspect.

Short answer. In a side-by-side, the freezer makes the cold and a damper, a small air door, meters how much of it reaches the refrigerator side. If the damper is stuck closed or its control has failed, the refrigerator side warms while the freezer stays cold. A failed evaporator fan, frost on the cooling coil and blocked vents look the same, so clear the vents and check the settings first. If the refrigerator side is still above 40°F after a day, call a technician.

How Cold Air Gets From the Freezer to the Refrigerator Side

On the refrigerators these repair guides cover, the cold is made in the freezer section, and the refrigerator side borrows it. A fan pushes air through supply vents into the fresh-food compartment, and return vents carry it back to be cooled again. Maytag Product Help describes the vents exactly that way: they move cold air from the freezer into the refrigerator and return it to be cooled again.

Between the two compartments sits the damper. Think of it as a door in the air path. PartSelect describes it as opening on a signal from the temperature control to let more cold air in, then closing to restrict the airflow once the target temperature is reached, AppliancePartsPros lists damper parts as controlling airflow between the freezer and refrigerator compartments, and iFixit describes it as a device that controls the flow of cold air from the freezer to the fresh food compartment on single-evaporator refrigerators. Where it sits and how it is driven vary by model, so your owner's manual is the place to look.

That is why the symptom splits the way it does. The freezer is where the cold is made, so it keeps working with the damper shut. The refrigerator side depends on air arriving through the damper. iFixit says that a damper that fails in the shut position makes the fresh food compartment warm up, and Maytag lists a damper stuck in the closed position among the causes when the freezer works but the fridge does not.

Causes of a Warm Refrigerator Side, in the Order We Rule Them Out

This is a checking order, not a ranking by how often each one happens. The guides we read disagree on which cause is most common, and the answer changes with the model.

1. Blocked or frosted vents

Cheapest to rule out, so it goes first. Maytag's product help is direct about it: if vents are blocked, airflow will decrease, and containers or bags placed directly in front of a vent can disrupt performance (vent guidance; too-warm guidance). Frigidaire's own support pages also say food should not block the air flow vents and that over-frosting can decrease airflow (Frigidaire support). Pull food a few inches back and look for frost on the vent openings.

2. Air damper control stuck closed or failed

If the vents are clear and the freezer is cold, the damper is the next stop. The clue is that cold air simply is not arriving: the refrigerator vents deliver little or none, with no frost to explain it. Replacing the damper control is a model-specific job. AppliancePartsPros lists Frigidaire damper parts under several different part numbers, so the model number decides which one fits. That is one reason we confirm the cause before a part is ordered.

3. A failed evaporator fan

The damper only opens a door; the fan is what pushes air through it. When the fan motor fails, air stops moving even though the freezer is making cold. iFixit advises checking the blade for obstructions, turning it by hand and replacing the motor if it does not spin freely, and Sears PartsDirect explains how to tell a fan blocked by frost from a failed motor. Both are technician-level checks on most models, since the fan sits behind a panel.

4. Frost from a defrost fault

Refrigerators periodically melt frost off the cooling coil. When the automatic defrost system fails to do that, frost builds until it can block the fan blades, and the refrigerator side warms. A loud buzzing noise from the freezer can mean the fan blades are hitting that frost. Thawing the unit buys a temporary reprieve only; Sears PartsDirect says the frost typically builds back within a day or two, and that a broken defrost system needs a permanent fix (Sears PartsDirect; iFixit).

5. Dirty condenser coils

Condenser coils shed the heat the system removes, and Maytag and iFixit both list dirty coils as a reason a refrigerator stops cooling well. Check your manual before pulling the unit out: GE says to follow the owner's manual directions if your model has coils that need cleaning, and to unplug the appliance before any cleaning. If yours are accessible, unplug the unit first.

6. A temperature sensor or the control board

The thermistor is a sensor that monitors the air temperature and reports to the control board, and iFixit says a defective one can keep the refrigerator from cooling. Maytag lists a faulty thermistor alongside the evaporator fan and a stuck damper for the warm-refrigerator, cold-freezer pattern. The control board comes last on purpose: iFixit says a sensor is usually more likely the problem than the temperature control board, and Maytag points to the circuit board once the other components check out.

Safe Checks Before You Call

  1. Read the real temperatures. Put an appliance thermometer in each compartment. The USDA's targets are 40°F or below for the refrigerator and 0°F or lower for the freezer (USDA), and Frigidaire support lists 0°F as the factory-suggested freezer setting.
  2. Change a setting only one step at a time. Maytag's guidance is to wait at least 24 hours between adjustments, because the temperatures take that long to settle (Maytag).
  3. Clear the vents. Move food back from every vent in both compartments and look for frost on the openings.
  4. Check the doors. Make sure a bin or shelf is not holding a door open, and that the gasket seals.
  5. Stop there. Pulling panels or testing parts on a live unit is a job for a technician. If you do clean accessible coils, unplug the refrigerator first.
Food safety: the USDA's chart says to discard perishable refrigerated food held above 40°F for more than two hours, and FoodSafety.gov says never to taste food to determine its safety (USDA; FoodSafety.gov). Those pages are written for power outages. Move perishables to a cooler with ice or into the freezer while you wait for the repair.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my Frigidaire refrigerator side not cooling when the freezer works?
The freezer makes the cold, and a fan and a damper carry some of it into the refrigerator side. If that delivery path is interrupted, the freezer stays cold while the refrigerator side warms. The usual suspects are blocked or frosted vents, a stuck or failed air damper control, a failed evaporator fan, frost from a defrost fault, and a bad temperature sensor. Clear the vents and check the settings first; if the refrigerator side is still above 40°F after about a day, a technician can find which part is at fault.
What does the air damper do in a side-by-side refrigerator?
It is a small air door between the freezer and the refrigerator side. It opens when the refrigerator side needs cold air and closes once it reaches its target, so it meters how much of the freezer's cold air gets through. Parts catalogs describe damper parts as controlling airflow between the two compartments. If it sticks closed, the refrigerator side runs warm while the freezer stays cold.
How can I tell a bad damper from a bad evaporator fan?
You usually cannot be certain without testing, which is why we measure before replacing anything. As a rough guide, a failed fan moves no air at all, which you can sometimes check by feeling for airflow at the vents on the freezer's back wall, while a stuck damper leaves the fan running but the refrigerator vents delivering little cold air. Frost on the freezer's back wall points to a defrost problem instead. Treat these as clues, not a diagnosis.
How long can food stay in a refrigerator that is not cooling?
The USDA's chart says to discard perishable refrigerated food held above 40°F for more than two hours, and FoodSafety.gov says never to taste food to determine its safety. An appliance thermometer in each compartment tells you the real temperature. Until the repair, move perishables to a cooler with ice or into the freezer if it has room. See the USDA guidance and FoodSafety.gov; both are written for power outages.
