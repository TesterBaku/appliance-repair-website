How Long Do Appliances Actually Last? 2026 Lifespan Data by Type and Brand

Home › Blog › Appliance Lifespan Data 2026

Most lifespan articles online give you one number per appliance: "refrigerators last 14 years." That number is a national average across every refrigerator from a $400 builder-grade unit to a $14,000 Sub-Zero. Useful for nothing. This article gives you three numbers per appliance, broken out by what you actually bought.

We anchored this data to the U.S. Department of Energy's calculated service-life figures, the most rigorous public numbers available, since service life is a required input to the cost-benefit analysis behind every federal appliance efficiency-standards rulemaking. We cross-referenced those against Consumer Reports' multi-year reliability surveys, manufacturer-published service-life specifications, and the appliances we actually service in Orange County, where premium brands run a higher share of the install base than the national average. (We're deliberately not treating the once-popular NAHB "Study of Life Expectancy of Home Components" as an independent source here: NAHB's only publication is from February 2007, and the InterNACHI chart some sites cite alongside it is itself built from NAHB's numbers, not a second study.) The result is a brand-tier lifespan table that reflects what each price point actually delivers.

Average Lifespan of the Major Appliances

Before the brand-tier breakdown, here is the all-brands national average from DOE and Consumer Reports, the two most rigorous public sources available. These numbers are useful as a starting point, but read on to the brand-tier table, where the spread within each appliance category is wider than the gap between appliance categories.

Average expected lifespan of major household appliances. Source: U.S. Department of Energy appliance efficiency-standards rulemakings, 2023-2024; Consumer Reports appliance buying guides
Appliance DOE mean service life Consumer Reports estimate Typical range in OC
Refrigerator 14.3 years ~10 years 11 to 18 years
Freezer (standard / compact) 18.4 / 11.3 years not separately published 12 to 22 years
Washing machine 13.4 years "at least a decade" 8 to 16 years
Clothes dryer (gas and electric) 14 years "a decade or so" 12 to 17 years
Dishwasher 15.2 years ~10 years 12 to 19 years
Cooking products (gas)* 14.5 years not separately published 13 to 17 years
Cooking products (electric)* 16.8 years not separately published 14 to 20 years
Microwave (over-the-range) 10.78 years not separately published 8 to 13 years

DOE figures are calculated mean service life from Weibull survival-curve fits to real shipment and housing-survey data in each rulemaking's life-cycle-cost analysis: refrigerators and freezers, 89 FR 3026 (17 January 2024); clothes washers, 89 FR 19026 (15 March 2024); clothes dryers, 89 FR 18164 (12 March 2024); dishwashers, 89 FR 31398 (24 April 2024); cooking products, 89 FR 11434 (14 February 2024); microwave ovens, 88 FR 39912 (20 June 2023). *Cooking-product figures are the exception: DOE built these from trade-association (AHAM) input rather than its own independent survival analysis, so treat them as softer numbers than the rest of the table. Consumer Reports publishes shorter, survey-based estimates for some categories (roughly a decade for refrigerators and dishwashers, "at least a decade" for washers, "a decade or so" for dryers); see the callout below for why the two disagree. DOE does not publish a service-life figure for garbage disposals, so that row has been dropped rather than reuse the older, unverifiable estimate. "Typical range in OC" reflects what our technicians see across the install base in Orange County, where premium brands and well-maintained units pull the upper bound higher than the national average.

How to read this table: DOE's mean service life is an engineering estimate of when half of all real-world units get retired, built from a Weibull survival curve fit to real shipment and housing-survey data, not a marketing number. Consumer Reports' shorter figures lean more on manufacturer specs and member-reported replacement habits, closer to "when people say they replace it" than "when the average unit statistically fails." Both are legitimate; they're measuring different things. Your actual lifespan depends heavily on which tier of brand you bought and whether you maintain it. The next table breaks that down.

Lifespan by Brand Tier: The Budget / Mid / Premium Breakdown

This is the centerpiece data point of this article: lifespan does not vary by 10 or 20 percent across brand tiers; it varies by 50 to 100 percent. A Miele dishwasher engineered for 20 years of average use is not a marginally better version of a $500 budget dishwasher. It is a fundamentally different product. The table below splits expected lifespan into three tiers so you can compare what you actually own (or are about to buy) against the right benchmark.

Washing machine (front-load) 7 to 10 10 to 13 15 to 20
Washing machine (top-load) 8 to 11 11 to 14 15 to 25

Budget tier: Amana, Hotpoint, entry-level GE and Frigidaire. Mid-range: Whirlpool, LG, Samsung, Bosch (standard lines), KitchenAid, GE Profile. Premium: Sub-Zero, Wolf, Miele, Thermador, Viking. Ranges synthesize Consumer Reports reliability surveys (2022 and 2024), Miele published 10,000-hour design specs, and Sub-Zero published warranty terms. Upper bound assumes consistent maintenance. Note that DOE's population-wide mean service life (previous table) sits at or above this table's mid-range band for some categories, dishwashers especially: DOE's Weibull survival-curve method captures the full population including long-surviving units, while the tier bands here lean on Consumer Reports' shorter, survey-based figures. Both are legitimate; they're measuring different things.

The honest caveat about premium lifespan: a 20-year Miele or Sub-Zero is a 20-year Miele or Sub-Zero only if you service it. Premium appliances stay running because their owners replace water filters, clean condenser coils, change door gaskets, and call a technician when something is off. A neglected premium appliance ages faster than a maintained mid-range one.

Per-Appliance Lifespan Deep Dive

Refrigerators

❄️
Refrigerators
DOE mean service life: 14.3 years. Premium built-in: 18 to 25 years.
Mid-range: 11 to 15 yrs Premium: 18 to 25 yrs Source: DOE 2024; Sub-Zero warranty terms

Refrigerators show the widest lifespan spread of any major appliance. DOE's population-wide mean of 14.3 years collapses three very different populations into one number: budget freestanding units that struggle to reach year 11, mid-range French-door models that typically deliver 12 to 14 years before sealed-system issues appear, and built-in Sub-Zero refrigerators that routinely run 20 years or more. Sub-Zero's 12-year sealed-system warranty is a direct statement of the company's confidence in compressor and condenser lifespan beyond what mid-range manufacturers will commit to.

The single biggest predictor of refrigerator longevity within any tier is condenser coil cleanliness. Energy Star data shows that dirty condenser coils force the compressor to run longer to maintain temperature, which is the leading cause of premature compressor failure. A condenser cleaning takes 15 minutes and meaningfully extends compressor life on any refrigerator regardless of tier. We cover the full maintenance routine in our refrigerator maintenance guide.

The same sealed-system arithmetic governs compressor-based wine coolers, which use the same compressor and condenser architecture in a smaller cabinet. A single-zone unit that drifts warm is usually showing the refrigeration fault described above, while dual-zone cabinets add failure points of their own, so either way it belongs in wine cooler repair rather than general refrigerator service.

Washing Machines

🫧
Washing Machines
DOE mean service life: 13.4 years. Front-load shorter, top-load longer.
Mid-range: 10 to 14 yrs Premium (Miele): 15 to 20 yrs Source: DOE 2024; Miele design spec; CR 2024

DOE's 13.4-year mean for washing machines still gets pulled down relative to the top of the tier range by two factors: the proliferation of front-load machines (which historically have shorter lifespans than top-loaders due to bearing and door-boot wear) and the budget tier expansion of the last decade. Mid-range front-loaders typically deliver 10 to 13 years; mid-range top-loaders 11 to 14. Miele explicitly publishes that its W1 washing machines are tested through 5,000 wash cycles, which the company describes as simulating 20 years of average household use. Real-world Miele washer lifespans of 15 to 20 years are widely reported by owners and Consumer Reports, with the caveat that Miele does not guarantee 20 years; only that the design target is that range.

Two maintenance tasks meaningfully extend washer lifespan: replacing rubber fill hoses every five years (the single most common catastrophic failure mode is a burst hose flooding the laundry room), and running a tub-clean cycle monthly to prevent biofilm in front-loaders.

Dryers

🌤️
Dryers (Gas and Electric)
DOE mean service life: 14 years. Among the most consistent across tiers.
Mid-range: 12 to 15 yrs Premium: 15 to 20 yrs Source: DOE 2024; CR 2024

Dryers are mechanically simple, which is why their lifespans are the most consistent across brand tiers. DOE's 14-year mean holds reasonably well across mid-range brands, with premium brands extending the upper bound by 3 to 7 years. The two factors that shorten dryer lifespan most reliably across any tier are restricted vent runs (long ducts with multiple elbows force the motor to work harder) and accumulated lint in the cabinet (a fire risk and a heat-buildup driver). Annual professional vent cleaning typically pays for itself within two years through extended motor and heating-element life.

Cost details by repair type for dryers are in our Orange County dryer repair cost guide.

Dishwashers

🍽️
Dishwashers
DOE mean service life: 15.2 years, well above the ~9-10 year figure older guides cite. Still the widest tier spread of any appliance.
Mid-range: 9 to 12 yrs Premium (Miele): 15 to 20 yrs Source: DOE 2024; Miele design spec

Dishwashers have the most surprising lifespan spread of any appliance category, and now the most surprising headline number. DOE's 2024 rulemaking puts the population-wide mean at 15.2 years, well above the roughly 9-to-10-year figure that older guides (including Consumer Reports, which estimates around 10 years) still cite. The two aren't measuring the same thing: DOE fits a survival curve to real shipment and housing-survey data, while Consumer Reports leans more on manufacturer specs and member-reported replacement habits. Either way, the tier spread is real. Miele dishwashers are engineered to a 20-year service life target, with stainless steel tubs, sealed motors, and serviceable internal components. Bosch's higher trim lines run 12 to 15 years reliably. Budget dishwashers in the $400 to $700 range typically deliver 6 to 9 years before control board or pump failures.

Water hardness is the most significant lifespan modifier for dishwashers across every tier. Orange County water averages 10 to 15 grains of hardness depending on city, which is on the higher end of moderate. Households on city water can extend dishwasher life by running a descaling cycle quarterly.

Ranges and Ovens

🍳
Ranges and Ovens
DOE mean service life: gas 14.5 years, electric 16.8 years (both AHAM-derived, so treat as softer numbers). Premium reaches 20+ years.
Mid-range gas: 13 to 17 yrs Premium (Wolf, Thermador): 20 to 25 yrs Source: DOE 2024 (AHAM-derived); CR 2024

Ranges and ovens are among the longest-lived major appliances. DOE's figures for this category come from trade-association (AHAM) input rather than DOE's own independent survival analysis, so they're softer than the other numbers in this article, and they actually show electric ranges (16.8 years) edging out gas (14.5 years), which is close enough that we wouldn't lean hard on the gap either way. Gas ranges still bring real mechanical simplicity to the kitchen (cast-iron burners, sealed gas valves, minimal electronics), and we routinely service Wolf ranges and Thermador units that are well past year 20 regardless of fuel type. Premium ranges also command strong resale values, which means the effective per-year cost is even lower than the lifespan numbers suggest. A Viking 48-inch range that originally cost $11,000 and is replaced at year 22 has an effective cost of roughly $500 per year, comparable to a mid-range freestanding refrigerator.

The two most common failure points across all range tiers are bake elements (electric) and burner igniters (gas). Both are inexpensive repairs that almost never argue for replacement.

Microwaves

⚡
Microwaves (Over-the-Range and Built-In)
DOE mean service life: 10.78 years. Still the shortest-lived major appliance category.
Mid-range: 8 to 10 yrs Premium: 10 to 14 yrs Source: DOE 2023

Microwaves have the shortest lifespans of any major appliance category. DOE's 10.78-year mean reflects a category where the magnetron, control board, door switches, and ventilation system are packed into a single compact unit, with limited serviceability. The premium tier extends lifespan by 3 to 5 years through better magnetron quality, more durable door switches, and serviceable control boards, but the underlying ceiling is lower than for any other category. A microwave past year 10 or 11 should generally be treated as approaching end of life regardless of brand.

How Lifespan Connects to Failure Rates

Lifespan is the headline number. Failure rate is the actuarial detail. The two are related but distinct: an appliance's expected lifespan is the year by which roughly half of comparable units will need replacement, while its annual failure rate is the probability that any given unit needs a repair in a specific year. Both follow the bathtub curve: elevated failure rates in year 1, low in the middle, rising in the wear-out phase.

For the full year-by-year failure probability tables and the per-appliance failure-spike thresholds, see our companion article on appliance failure rates by year. The two articles together form a complete picture: this article tells you how long an appliance is expected to last; the failure-rate article tells you in which year a repair is most likely to be needed within that lifespan.

The practical pairing: if your appliance is in the middle third of its expected lifespan, a one-off repair is almost always worth it. If it's in the final third, cross-check against the failure-rate table to see whether the next failure is statistically likely within 2 years.

When the Data Argues for Replacement vs. Repair

Lifespan data sharpens repair-vs-replace decisions in three specific ways:

  • Tier match. A 9-year-old budget dishwasher (the 6-to-9-year tier above) is at or near the end of its statistical lifespan; replacement is usually the right call. A 9-year-old Miele dishwasher is at roughly the halfway point of its statistical lifespan; repair almost always wins. This uses the tier-specific figures above, not the DOE population-wide mean of 15.2 years discussed earlier, which averages across every tier and runs considerably higher.
  • Maintenance history. A well-maintained unit can reasonably be expected to reach the upper bound of its tier range. A unit with no maintenance history should be benchmarked against the lower bound.
  • Configuration complexity. Within any tier, more complex configurations (French-door refrigerators vs top-freezers, front-load washers vs top-loaders, induction cooktops vs gas burners) trend toward the lower lifespan bound because they have more failure points.

Frequently Asked Questions

Major household appliances have DOE-calculated mean service lives ranging from about 11 to 18 years depending on type. According to the U.S. Department of Energy's 2023-2024 efficiency-standards rulemakings, refrigerators last 14.3 years on average, washing machines 13.4 years, dryers 14 years, dishwashers 15.2 years, gas ranges about 14.5 years and electric ranges about 16.8 years (both cooking-product figures are AHAM-derived, not DOE's own survival analysis), and microwaves 10.78 years. (These figures run notably higher than older, unverifiable NAHB-sourced estimates that circulated for years; DOE's Weibull survival-curve method captures more of the real-world population than survey-based estimates do.) Premium brands like Sub-Zero, Miele, and Wolf are designed for 20-year service lives. Actual lifespan in any household depends on usage frequency, maintenance, water quality, and the original build tier.
Premium-tier brands consistently outlast mid-range and budget brands. Miele explicitly designs its washing machines and dishwashers for 10,000 hours of operation, which the company describes as roughly 20 years of average household use. Sub-Zero built-in refrigerators are widely reported to operate 18 to 25 years with proper service, and the company backs them with a 12-year sealed-system warranty. Wolf ranges and Thermador cooktops routinely reach 20 years in residential use. Among mid-range brands, Bosch and Speed Queen show the strongest lifespan data. Budget brands typically deliver 7 to 11 years depending on appliance type.
Three factors compress modern appliance lifespans. First, electronic control boards have replaced mechanical timers and switches, and the boards fail more frequently than the parts they replaced. Second, energy efficiency standards have pushed manufacturers toward smaller motors, thinner gauge metals, and more complex internal water and air paths, all of which add failure modes. Third, the budget tier has expanded: appliances built to a $400 to $700 price point use lighter components than the equivalent units sold at the same price (inflation-adjusted) in the 1990s. Premium brands have largely resisted this trend, which is why their lifespan numbers have stayed stable.

Replacing washing machine fill hoses every five years prevents the single most common catastrophic failure (a burst hose flooding the laundry room). Cleaning dryer vents annually reduces motor and heating element load and reduces fire risk. The general estimate across appliance-industry maintenance guidance is that consistent maintenance extends appliance lifespan by 20 to 40 percent versus neglected units, though the exact figure varies by appliance type and household conditions.
For long-term homeowners, the math usually favors premium. A Sub-Zero refrigerator at $10,000 amortized over 22 years costs about $455 per year of service. A $1,800 mid-range French door refrigerator amortized over 11 years costs about $164 per year. The premium unit costs roughly 2.8x per year of service, but it also delivers higher build quality, lower failure rates during ownership (per Yale Appliance reliability data), and significantly higher kitchen resale appeal. For owners who plan to stay in a home 15 or more years, premium is usually defensible. For shorter ownership windows, mid-range brands often win on total cost of ownership.
