Miele Washer Not Draining? Causes, DIY Checks and Repair Costs

Home › Blog › Miele Washer Not Draining

You open the door after the cycle finishes and the drum is still sitting in a couple of inches of gray water. On a Miele front-loader, a washer that won't drain almost always comes down to one of five things: a clogged drain filter, a kinked or blocked drain hose, a failed drain pump, a stuck check valve, or a pressure sensor that's telling the control board the wrong story. This guide ranks each cause by how often we see it, walks through the safe way to open the filter yourself, and lays out honest repair cost ranges for Miele owners across Orange County.

Before you open the filter, read this. Cancel the program and unplug the washer, never just switch it off, before you open the drain filter flap. Water will spill out when you loosen the filter cap, typically about half a gallon under normal conditions and noticeably more if the main drain hose is also clogged, so set a shallow tray and a few towels down first and unscrew the cap slowly in stages rather than all at once. F11 vs. the WaterProof System: What's Actually Wrong

Every Miele front-loader, from the current W1 lineup (model prefixes like WWB, WWD, WWF, WKF, and WDB) back through the older W Classic series, has a drain filter you can reach yourself behind a small flap at the bottom-front-left of the cabinet. Miele recommends cleaning it every 6 to 8 weeks, and a clogged filter is by far the most common reason water gets left in the drum, often something you can clear for free in a few minutes.

When the machine's own diagnostics flag a drain problem, older W Classic models display it as Fault F11, Miele's code for "the drain is restricted." That's a filter-or-hose blockage, not an electrical fault. Newer W1 models tend to show the same underlying issue as a plain-language drain alert instead of a numeric code, but it means the same thing.

Don't confuse either of those with Miele's WaterProof system. That's a separate safety feature built around a float switch in the machine's base pan; it trips when water is detected where it shouldn't be, usually from an internal leak, and shuts off the water supply as a precaution. A WaterProof trip is a leak problem, not a drainage problem, and it calls for a different repair path entirely. If your display is warning about WaterProof, start there instead of chasing the drain filter.

Causes Ranked by Likelihood

#1 Most Common

Clogged Drain Filter

The drain filter catches everything the drum's inlet screen misses: coins, hairpins, bra underwires, pet hair, and the fine lint that builds up load after load. It sits behind a small hinged flap on the bottom-front-left of the cabinet, and it's designed for owner access, unlike most of the parts further inside the machine.

Symptoms: water left standing in the drum at the end of a cycle; a cycle that stalls or repeats at the spin step; a gurgling or straining sound during drain; a drain alert or Fault F11 on the display.

DIY check: with the washer off and unplugged, open the bottom flap, pull the small emergency drain hose down into a tray, and let it empty before you touch the main filter cap. Then unscrew the large cap slowly, in stages, catching the water that follows. Clear out anything you find and spin the impeller behind the filter opening by hand to confirm it turns freely before reinstalling.

Repair cost: In most cases, nothing beyond the visit itself. Clearing a clogged filter takes a few minutes and rarely needs parts.

#2

Kinked or Blocked Drain Hose (Fault F11)

If the filter is clear and water still won't leave the drum, the drain hose itself is the next place to look. A hose pinched behind the machine, coiled too tightly, or run at the wrong height can restrict flow just as effectively as a clogged filter, and a blocked or narrowed standpipe on the wall side does the same thing from the other end. This is the failure Miele's Fault F11 is actually describing.

Symptoms: slow draining rather than a complete stop; gurgling partway through the cycle; Fault F11 on W Classic models, or a plain-language drain warning on W1 models.

DIY check: pull the washer out enough to see the full hose run and straighten any kinks or tight bends. Disconnect the hose at the standpipe end and look for a blockage or buildup inside, and confirm the hose isn't looped higher than your installation manual calls for.

Repair cost: $150 to $300. Estimates vary by brand, part availability, and diagnosis. Final quote is provided before repair.

#3

Drain Pump Failure

Once the filter and hose have both checked out clear, the drain pump itself becomes the likely suspect. A dead pump motor produces total silence during the drain step, no hum, no whir, nothing. A jammed impeller or a worn-out bearing produces the opposite problem: an audible grinding or humming noise with no actual water movement. Genuine Miele pump assemblies are priced at a premium compared to mainstream brands.

Symptoms: no pump sound at all during drain; a grinding or straining hum with water still standing in the drum; a burning-plastic smell in rare cases where the motor windings have failed.

Confirmation: a technician checks for a foreign object jammed in the pump housing first, since that's often mistaken for a dead motor, then tests the pump's electrical draw and impeller movement directly.

Repair cost: $250 to $450. Estimates vary by brand, part availability, and diagnosis. Final quote is provided before repair.

#4

Non-Return (Check) Valve

A non-return valve in the drain path is supposed to let water flow out and then stay shut, so drained water can't creep back into the drum once the cycle is finished. When that valve sticks or wears out, water siphons backward from the standpipe or drain line after the machine has already finished and shut off.

Symptoms: the drum is dry right when a cycle ends, but has an inch or two of water in it if you check back 15 to 30 minutes later; an occasional sewer-like smell from the drum between loads.

Confirmation test: run an empty cycle and check the drum immediately at the finish, then check it again half an hour later. Water that reappears on its own, with the machine fully off, points to the check valve rather than the pump or filter.

Repair cost: $150 to $300, the same range as a drain hose repair, since the valve is typically part of the same hose assembly. Estimates vary by brand, part availability, and diagnosis. Final quote is provided before repair.

#5

Pressure Sensor or Control Board Fault

The pressure sensor's job is to tell the control board when the water level has actually dropped so the board knows the drain step succeeded. A kinked sensor hose or a sensor that has drifted out of calibration can report the wrong water level even while the pump is doing its job correctly, so the board aborts the cycle or repeats the drain step anyway.

Symptoms: the pump audibly runs and the drain hose feels full during the cycle, but the washer won't advance past the drain or spin step; the cycle repeats the same step over and over.

Confirmation: a technician checks the sensor hose for kinks or moisture first, since that's the simpler fix, before testing the sensor itself. Recalibrating the pressure sensor on some Miele models requires Miele's own diagnostic software, which is why this one isn't a homeowner fix.

Repair cost: $180 to $350 for a sensor or hose repair; $400 to $700 if the control board itself needs replacing. Estimates vary by brand, part availability, and diagnosis. Final quote is provided before repair.

DIY Checks You Can Do Today

Run through these before you book a visit. Most water-left-in-the-drum complaints on a Miele washer trace back to something you can check, and often fix, without any tools.

  • Clean the drain filter first. Unplug the washer, open the bottom flap, drain the small emergency hose into a tray, then slowly unscrew the main filter cap and clear out any debris.
  • Check the drain hose for kinks. Pull the machine out and look at the full hose run for pinches, tight coils, or a height that doesn't match your installation manual.
  • Run a drain-only or spin-only cycle. If the program menu has a standalone drain or spin option, running it tells you immediately whether water moves at all.
  • Listen during the drain step. A completely silent pump points to a dead motor; a grinding or straining hum with no water movement points to a jammed impeller.
  • Watch for water reappearing in a finished, empty drum. Check right at cycle end and again 15 to 30 minutes later. Water that comes back on its own signals the check valve, not the pump or filter.
  • Write down the exact message or code. A Fault F11 versus a WaterProof warning point a technician toward two completely different repairs.
Maintenance tip: Cleaning the drain filter every 6 to 8 weeks, as Miele recommends, catches most clogs before they ever cause a full stoppage. Emptying pockets before loading, using a mesh bag for small items like hair clips and bra underwires, and keeping loose coins and keys off the load are the habits that keep this from becoming a recurring problem.

Pro Repair Scope

A few of these fixes are reasonable to tackle yourself; the rest are better left to a technician.

Drain filter cleaning: Fully DIY. Miele built the filter to be owner-accessible, and the only real risk is the water spill if you rush the filter cap open instead of unscrewing it slowly.

Drain hose and standpipe inspection: Reasonable for a confident homeowner. Pulling the machine out, checking the hose for kinks, and clearing a blocked standpipe don't require disassembling the washer itself.

Drain pump, check valve, pressure sensor, and control board work: These call for opening the cabinet, and in some cases removing the front kick panel or rear service panel, to reach the part. Pressure-sensor recalibration in particular needs Miele's proprietary diagnostic software on certain models, and any work near the sealed electrical housing should stay with a technician.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I drain a Miele washing machine manually, and where is the drain filter?

Cancel the program and unplug the washer first, don't just switch it off. Open the small flap at the bottom front of the machine, usually on the left side, and pull out the short emergency drain hose into a shallow tray to let the bulk of the water out. Then slowly unscrew the larger filter cap counterclockwise, emptying it in stages rather than all at once. The filter is designed to be owner-accessible, and Miele recommends cleaning it every 6 to 8 weeks as routine maintenance.

Why is there water left in my Miele washer drum after the cycle ends?

Most often it's a clogged drain filter or a kinked or blocked drain hose, which is exactly what Miele's Fault F11 ("the drain is restricted") is warning you about. Less commonly it's a failed drain pump, a non-return valve letting drained water siphon back into the drum, or a pressure sensor issue confusing the control board. Start with the filter since it's the most common cause and the easiest to check yourself.

What does a drain-related fault message (F11) mean on my Miele washer?

On older W Classic models it shows up as Fault F11, Miele's code for a restricted drain. Newer W1 models tend to display the same underlying issue as a plain-language message instead of a numeric code, but it means the same thing: water isn't leaving the drum fast enough. The fix path is the same either way, check the filter first, then the hose and standpipe.

Estimates vary by brand, part availability, and diagnosis. Final quote is provided before repair.

How often should I clean the drain filter on a Miele washer, and can I damage it doing it myself?

Miele recommends cleaning the drain filter about every 6 to 8 weeks. It's built for owner access, so the risk of doing it yourself is minimal as long as you unplug the washer first and unscrew the cap slowly to control the water spill. Skipping this routine cleaning is the single most common reason we get called out for a washer that won't drain.
